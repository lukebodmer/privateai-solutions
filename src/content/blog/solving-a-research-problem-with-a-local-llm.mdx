A neuro-oncology lab I was working with had the following problem. They had a
bunch of patient data folders that contained MRI and CT images of a patient
over time, a JSON file that they knew somehow represented the coordinates of
nervous tissue biopsy points in those MRI and CT scans, and a ground truth
snapshot showing the biopsy points in one of those scans. The problem was that
the data was from a proprietary surgical-navigation system, and no one could
interpret the JSON file to know exactly where those points sat in each of the
images. Each image had the same patient's head in it, but the coordinate
systems and orientations were all slightly different.

They asked me if I could figure out how to interpret the JSON data to find the
biopsy points in each of the patient's MRI and CT scans.

I thought this would make a good case study to show what a language
model running on limited hardware could do. A problem like
this can't go to the cloud, because the data is full of protected health
information (PHI). PHI is trickier than it might seem. It is not only names and
dates of birth in the file headers. A head MRI contains the patient's face,
and software can rebuild that face from the scan well enough to recognize the
person. The scans are exactly what you need to solve the problem, and the lab
can't send them to a cloud AI service and still keep its obligation of privacy to
its patients.

When I read through the JSON, I noticed it was full of 4×4 matrices that
suggested 3D transformation matrices. The DICOM folder contained many MRI and CT
scans, and each scan had its own coordinate system. My guess was that the JSON
wrote the biopsy points in one of those scans' coordinate systems and used the
matrices to get from that scan to each of the others.

## The problem

I set up Neuman on a 3090 GPU using the [Qwen 3.8 27B](/models/qwen3.8-27b)
model with a 4-bit quantization, gave it the DICOM images, the JSON file,
and a Python environment with `matplotlib`, `numpy`, `pydicom`, `scipy`, and
`simpleitk`, and the following prompt:

<Callout title="The prompt">
You have a DICOM folder of MRI and CT scans of one patient, taken at many
different times. You have a JSON file that we think does two things: it gives
the biopsy points in the coordinate system of one of those scans, and it gives
a way to get from that scan's coordinate system to each of the others.

You also have one piece of ground truth: a screenshot from the navigation
system, showing a biopsy point on the axial, coronal and sagittal slices of
the MRI. Whatever answer we reach has to put the point in the same place as the screenshot.
</Callout>

## What it found

After about thirty minutes of talking the problem through with Neuman, I had
these facts:

- **The JSON names a reference scan.** One scan's coordinate system is marked
  as the reference, and the biopsy points are written in it.
- **Each 4×4 matrix maps the reference scan into one other scan.** Every other
  scan has its own rigid transformation from the reference frame into its
  frame.
- **The answer matches the ground truth.** I used the matrices to bring every
  scan into the reference frame, then looked at the axial, coronal and
  sagittal slices of the MRI at each biopsy point. They matched the
  screenshot from the navigation system.

Neuman had also correctly found where each of the biopsy points is located in
the coordinate system of each image.

## What this means

The principal investigator of the lab told me that several people had tried to
answer this question before, and no one had managed it. Before AI, this was a
genuinely difficult problem. A person well enough versed in math could solve
it, but it would require a lot of trial and error and some pretty sophisticated
programming skills. That's the beauty of local AI. You get an extremely capable
assistant and you don't have to worry about where your data goes, who will see
it, or who will keep it and use it for their own purposes. You can put a model
to work on the difficult, tedious problems, and solve them quickly to move on
to the more exciting work.
